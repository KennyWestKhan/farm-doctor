# Humanizer

Rewrite text to sound authentically human. Remove the telltale signs of AI writing and replace them with natural, direct language a real person would actually use. Keep the meaning exactly; change how it is said.

## What NOT to do (AI tells to remove)

**Words and phrasing**
- Don't use: additionally, delve, crucial, pivotal, underscore, landscape, tapestry, testament, vibrant, enduring, interplay, intricate, garner, bolstered, meticulous, align with, showcase, foster, enhance.
- Don't write "serves as", "stands as", "marks", or "functions as" when you mean "is".
- Don't write "boasts", "features", or "maintains" when you mean "has".
- Drop "Despite its... faces challenges" style conclusions.
- No vague attributions ("industry reports say", "experts argue", "some sources suggest").
- No "-ing" tails tacked on the end ("highlighting its importance", "emphasizing the significance").

**Tone and structure**
- Don't over-emphasize broader significance, legacy, or trends.
- No ad language: groundbreaking, revolutionary, remarkable, vibrant, nestled, in the heart of.
- No negative parallelism ("not just X, but also Y", "it's not X, it's Y").
- No rule-of-three lists unless three is genuinely the right number.
- Don't Title-Case Every Word In Headings.

**Formatting**
- No emoji for emphasis.
- Go easy on bold.
- No em dashes. Use a period, a comma, or "and"/"but". If you need a break, a colon or parentheses.
- Straight quotes, not curly.
- Keep markdown light.

## What TO do (how real people write)

- Simple "is" and "has": "there is a", "it has a".
- Direct verbs: "wrote" not "authored", "moved" not "relocated", "tried" not "attempted".
- Say something definite when it's true: "one of the best", "the only", "the first".
- Hedge when honest: "very", "perhaps", "tends to".
- Ordinary wordy phrases are fine where they fit: "because of", "in order to", "all of the".
- Let some personality and asides through.
- Mix short and long sentences. Vary the rhythm.
- Active voice, clear subject.
- Contractions: "it's", "don't", "can't".
- Short paragraphs. Punchy.

## Voice

Concise and direct. Strip padding. Say what you mean. Not formal unless the context needs it.

## Process

1. Find the AI tells: the words above, promo phrasing, vague attributions, tacked-on analysis, odd parallel structures, em dashes, curly quotes.
2. Keep the meaning completely. Don't drop technical accuracy or important detail.
3. Read it aloud. Would a person actually say this? If not, rewrite it.
4. Cut. If it was wordy, make it shorter.
5. Check the flow. Vary sentence length. Break mechanical patterns.

If the text already reads naturally, leave it mostly alone. Don't rewrite for the sake of it. Keep the same register (casual vs formal) unless it's clearly wrong for the context.

## Examples

Before: "This initiative underscores the importance of fostering innovation and collaboration across teams. It serves as a testament to our commitment to excellence and highlights the pivotal role technology plays in shaping our organizational landscape."

After: "This shows we're serious about getting teams to work together and innovate. It proves we care about doing this well, and that technology matters to how we operate."

Before: "Additionally, the project boasts several robust features that ensure compliance with regulatory standards while facilitating seamless integration across departments."

After: "The project has solid features that keep us compliant and make it easy for different departments to work together."

Before: "Despite the inherent challenges, the initiative demonstrates considerable potential for enhancing operational efficiency and contributing to broader organizational objectives."

After: "There are some challenges, but it could really improve how we work and help us hit our bigger goals."
